# GTA-style two-wheelers

A city or road bicycle and a street motorbike or scooter, for the player to ride, in a GTA 3 / Vice City / San Andreas
spirit and light enough for phones. The wheels and the steering assembly (fork plus handlebars) must be separate
pieces; the bicycle also needs its crank with pedals separate, because the pedalling is animated exactly. A rider pose
is a bonus.

Budget per model: at most 300 KB (glb with textures) and 5,000 triangles.

## How this was researched

- Sources searched: poly.pizza (14 search terms), OpenGameArt (advanced search, 3D art), Quaternius, Kenney, Poly Haven
  (asset API), itch.io (free, CC0, PSX and low-poly asset searches), GitHub code search, and Sketchfab (public API
  `api.sketchfab.com/v3/search`, downloadable only, under 12,000 faces, 50 search terms). About 230 two-wheelers were
  listed. Some 150 were compared on contact sheets of their previews, and 30 were downloaded or checked in detail.
- **Measured** means downloaded and opened. A bun script reads the glb JSON chunk. It counts triangles per mesh node and
  lists the node tree, images, skins and animations. `.blend`, `.fbx` and `.obj` sources were opened in Blender 4
  (headless). Each was exported to glb and rendered once to check the look. Its per-part bounds and pivots were then
  read. Size cuts were tried with `gltf-transform` 4.5.1 (`resize`, `webp`, `meshopt`), and the result was rendered
  again to compare.
- **Stated** means read from the source: Sketchfab's search data, which gives the triangle count and its own glb
  archive size, or the author's page. Sketchfab downloads need an account or API token, and none was available. No
  Sketchfab model was measured, and none of their part splits could be seen.
- Licences were read on each source page itself: the poly.pizza model page (its licence line and link), OpenGameArt's
  licence field, the itch.io page (its "Asset license" row or description), and the Sketchfab model page, rendered in
  headless Chrome (its `License:` block and CC link). Where a licence file ships in the download, it was read too.
- itch.io free files have no stable direct URL. A script can still fetch them without an account: it POSTs
  `<page>/download_url`, opens the returned download page, then POSTs `<page>/file/<upload_id>?source=game_download`
  in the same cookie session. This was done here with Playwright (`bun`); the Spinner repository's copy was used.
- Ripped game assets were dropped on sight: the "Dave Mirra Freestyle BMX" levels, "Shenmue - Bicycle",
  "Fusionfall-Bicycle", "Pcj600-gta Vcs", "Mgs4 Motocycle" and the Silent Hill meshes.

## Ranking

### Bicycle

1. **PSX Vehicles Free: bicycle by heyheythere** (CC BY 4.0, itch.io): a red PS1-style city bike. It has realistic
   proportions, a rack, a lamp and a kickstand, and one point-filtered texture. Its hierarchy is exactly what the
   pedalling needs. The crank (with pedals) is its own node, and the fork (with the handlebars) is a node that carries
   the front wheel. The rear wheel is a third node, and `ride` and `steer` clips are included. It measured 984
   triangles and 159 KB with its texture embedded.
2. **Bicycle by Clint Bellanger** (CC-BY 3.0, OpenGameArt): a rigged toon bicycle of 820 triangles with a `Pedaling`
   clip. It has one bone per moving part: `Steering`, `WheelFront`, `WheelBack`, `CrankArm`, `PedalLeft` and
   `PedalRight`. It is the only candidate whose pedals turn level against the crank. Its chunky BMX-like look is far
   from GTA, though.
3. **Rusty Bicycle by Piotr "Pjot" Dyderski** (CC BY 4.0, Sketchfab): the most San Andreas-looking bicycle found, a
   photo-textured old roadster. It is stated at 2,655 triangles. Its 15 MB archive is all texture. Its parts could not
   be checked without a Sketchfab account.

#### Recommendation

Take heyheythere's bicycle. It is the only candidate that passes every test at once: the GTA-era textured look, real
proportions, and a part tree built for this exact job (crank with pedals, fork with bars and front wheel, rear wheel,
each pivoting on its own node). It is 3 times under the triangle budget and about half the size budget as shipped.
`webp` brings it to 100 KB and `meshopt` to 50 KB. The pedals are fixed to the crank, so they tip over with it. If the
rider's feet must stay level on them, split the two pedal quads into their own nodes in Blender: a ten-minute job on a
984-triangle mesh. Credit it with the line its licence file asks for. Two things to weigh: the page states the pack was
"Made with AI assistance (code, text, models and textures, all generated by code)". Its 1024 px atlas is also shared
with the rest of the pack, so it could be cropped to the bicycle's region. Keep Clint Bellanger's rig in mind as the
reference for the pedalling bones. Keep the Rusty Bicycle as the look benchmark, worth a Sketchfab login if the PS1
bike ever reads as too plain.

### Motorbike or scooter

1. **Another PSX Style Low-poly Bike by Duhgless** (CC0, itch.io): a PS2-era chopper with a flame-painted tank. It is
   pure San Andreas "Freeway". It has four nodes, `Cube` (frame), `Fork`, `Front-Wheel` and `Back-Wheel`, and each
   wheel pivots on its axle. It measured 3,968 triangles and 2,231 KB. Resized to 256 px textures and `webp`, it is
   262 KB with no visible loss in the render. With `meshopt` as well, it is 103 KB.
2. **Yet Another PSX Style Low-poly Bike by Duhgless** (CC0, itch.io): the same hand, as a lean café-style street bike
   with a sissy bar. It has the same split: `Fonk-Frame`, `Forks`, `FrontWheel` and `BackWheel`. It measured 3,828
   triangles and 2,129 KB, which is 259 KB after 256 px and `webp`, and 100 KB with `meshopt`.
3. **Old Scooter by Nadia Ribitis** (CC BY 4.0, Sketchfab): a photo-textured 1976 Vespa Primavera with a crate on the
   back. It is the Faggio of the list. It is stated at 1,793 triangles, and its archive of 13.3 MB is six textures. The
   parts are unknown without a Sketchfab account.

#### Recommendation

Take Duhgless's "Another PSX Style Low-poly Bike" (Bike3) as the motorbike. It is CC0, it already looks like it rolled
out of a 2004 Grove Street garage, and its fork and both wheels are their own nodes, with the wheels on their axles. The
one change it needs is texture size: the four 1024 px PNGs make up 2 MB. At 256 px `webp` they keep the look and bring
the file to 262 KB, under budget, or 103 KB with `meshopt`. Two small fixes are needed in the scene, not the file. The
fork's node is not tilted to the steering head, so wrap it in a group rotated to the rake before turning it. The model
is also about 2.5 times life size (its front wheel is 1.66 units across), so scale it by about 0.4. Fonk-Bike is the
natural second machine, a lean street bike from the same set. If the city wants a scooter's buzz rather than a
chopper's rumble, the Old Scooter is worth a Sketchfab login: resized to 512 px, it should fit the budget (inferred from
its six textures and 1,793 triangles).

### Rider pose

- No free model was found with a rider in a riding pose that also fits the brief. The riders found ("Low-pol Bike
  Rider" male and female by newArteest, CC BY 4.0, 674 and 696 triangles stated; "80's mystery biker",
  CC BY 4.0) stand in a T-pose.
- Clint Bellanger's bicycle rig is the nearest thing to a riding pose: its `CrankArm` and pedal bones give the feet
  their exact path. A rider on any of these bikes will be the project's person rig, posed in code.

  Scooter" ($3.99; it does have a steering setup), Akochan's 15-bike pack ($2.99), Se1dev's motorcycle pack ($2.99),
  Bukkbeek's "Motorbike Adventure" ($1.49).

  Scooter" is a kick scooter, and the many e-scooters are kick scooters too.

## Gaps and open checks

- The two Sketchfab picks (Rusty Bicycle, Old Scooter) need a Sketchfab account or API token. Their part splits and
  real sizes are unverified until then.
- None of the measured motorbikes has the fork tilted to its steering head. Every one needs a rake-tilted pivot group
  in three.js.
- heyheythere's pedals turn with the crank rather than staying level. Clint Bellanger's rig is the only free model
  found with level-keeping pedal bones.
